## John Wesley Dobbs (1882-1961)

### Takeaway
John Wesley Dobbs was a Marietta-born railway mail clerk and Grand Master of Georgia's Prince Hall Masons. He founded the Atlanta Civic and Political League (1936), co-founded the Atlanta Negro Voters League with A. T. Walden, and coined the name "Sweet Auburn." He died on August 30, 1961, is buried at South-View, and was the grandfather of Mayor Maynard Jackson.

### Cited Findings
**Identity, birth, death**
- Name: John Wesley Dobbs. Nickname: "the Grand," earned after his election as Grand Master of the Prince Hall Masons — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Called the "unofficial mayor of Auburn Avenue" — [SYM, quoting AJC](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
- Born in 1882 in Marietta, Georgia, to Minnie and Will Dobbs. His parents separated when he was about two — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Suffered a stroke on August 21, 1961, and died nine days later, on August 30, 1961 — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Age at death: 79 — [KING](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/king-papers/documents/invocation-delivered-funeral-john-wesley-dobbs-big-bethel-ame-church-0)

**Funeral and burial**
- Funeral held September 2, 1961, at Big Bethel AME Church, Atlanta. Martin Luther King Jr. gave the invocation, and William Holmes Borders of Wheat Street Baptist gave the eulogy — [KING](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/king-papers/documents/invocation-delivered-funeral-john-wesley-dobbs-big-bethel-ame-church-0)
- King spoke at the funeral and Thurgood Marshall was a pallbearer — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Buried at South-View Cemetery. CBS lists him among the civic leaders buried there, calling him grand master of the Prince Hall Masons and grandfather of Maynard Jackson — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/south-view-cemetery-ensures-over-a-century-of-black-atlantas-history-isnt-forgotten/)
- Patch also lists him as having a South-View grave site — [Patch](https://patch.com/georgia/cascade/south-view-cemetery-celebrating-125-years) (taken from a search-result summary; the page itself was not opened)

**Family**
- Married Irene Ophelia Thompson in 1906 — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Six daughters, all Spelman College graduates. Mattiwilda, the fifth, became an opera singer — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Grandson Maynard Jackson became Atlanta's mayor — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/). CBS calls Jackson Atlanta's first Black mayor — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/south-view-cemetery-ensures-over-a-century-of-black-atlantas-history-isnt-forgotten/)
- The King family moved to Boulevard near the Dobbs home in 1941, and the two families' children played together — [KING](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/king-papers/documents/invocation-delivered-funeral-john-wesley-dobbs-big-bethel-ame-church-0)

**Education**
- Attended school full time in Savannah from 1891 — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Enrolled at Atlanta Baptist College (later Morehouse) in 1897 but left to care for his ill mother. He earned no degree and kept studying on his own — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)

**Timeline**
- 1903: Passed the civil service exam and became a U.S. Post Office railway mail clerk in Atlanta. He held the job for 32 years — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- 1906: Married Irene Ophelia Thompson — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- 1911: Initiated into the Prince Hall Masons — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- 1932: Elected Grand Master of the Prince Hall Masons of Georgia — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/). The King Papers give his tenure as 1932-1961 — [KING](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/king-papers/documents/invocation-delivered-funeral-john-wesley-dobbs-big-bethel-ame-church-0)
- 1936: Founded the Atlanta Civic and Political League — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- Voter registration: worked toward registering 10,000 Black voters in Atlanta — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/). CBS credits him with registering over 10,000 African Americans in Georgia — [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/south-view-cemetery-ensures-over-a-century-of-black-atlantas-history-isnt-forgotten/)
- 1948: Helped win the hiring of eight Black police officers, who were limited to patrolling Black neighborhoods — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- 1949: Helped win street lamps for Auburn Avenue — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
- July 7, 1949: The Atlanta Negro Voters League (ANVL) was founded at the Butler Street YMCA as a bipartisan group. Dobbs, a Republican who led the Fulton County Republican Club, was one of its first co-chairs alongside A. T. Walden — [NGE-ANVL](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/government-politics/atlanta-negro-voters-league-anvl/)
  - **CONFLICT on the ANVL founding year:** the NGE Dobbs biography says he co-founded the league with Walden in **1946** — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/). The NGE ANVL article gives **July 7, 1949** — [NGE-ANVL](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/government-politics/atlanta-negro-voters-league-anvl/). The Atlanta History Center also supports 1949 — [AHC](https://www.atlantahistorycenter.com/blog/atlanta-negro-voters-league-strength-in-unity/) (from a search summary; page not opened). Wikipedia's Dobbs biography says 1946. **1949 is better supported.** The 1946 date may be confused with the 1946 federal ruling that struck down Georgia's white primary, which the NGE ANVL article mentions.
- ANVL work included voter registration committees, candidate screening, slates published in the *Atlanta Daily World* and announced on WERD, and rides to the polls. It backed Mayor William B. Hartsfield in 1949, 1953 and 1957 — [NGE-ANVL](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/government-politics/atlanta-negro-voters-league-anvl/)
- 1953: Split with the ANVL because no Black firemen had been hired, and withdrew his Republican supporters. Rev. William Jackson replaced him in 1954 — [NGE-ANVL](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/government-politics/atlanta-negro-voters-league-anvl/)
- Coined the term "Sweet Auburn" for the Auburn Avenue area (no year given) — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)

**Honors and legacy**
- January 10, 1994: His grandson, Mayor Maynard Jackson, renamed Houston Street as John Wesley Dobbs Avenue — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)

**Facts a visitor might ask about**
1. He gave Auburn Avenue the name "Sweet Auburn" — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
2. He was the grandfather of Maynard Jackson, Atlanta's first Black mayor, who later renamed a street John Wesley Dobbs Avenue in his honor (1994) — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/); [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/south-view-cemetery-ensures-over-a-century-of-black-atlantas-history-isnt-forgotten/)
3. Martin Luther King Jr. gave the invocation at his 1961 funeral, and Thurgood Marshall was a pallbearer — [KING](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/king-papers/documents/invocation-delivered-funeral-john-wesley-dobbs-big-bethel-ame-church-0); [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)
4. All six of his daughters graduated from Spelman — [NGE-JWD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/john-wesley-dobbs-1882-1961/)

### Inferences
- If the 32-year mail-clerk career began in 1903, it would have ended around 1935, just before he founded the Atlanta Civic and Political League in 1936. NGE calls him a "retired postal employee" by 1949.

### Gaps
- Exact birth date: NGE gives only the year 1882. Wikipedia gives March 26, 1882, but I did not verify it with an authoritative source.
- Place of death: NGE does not state it.
- No official South-View burial record or plot location was found. Burial is confirmed by CBS Atlanta and by Patch (search summary only).

## Mattiwilda Dobbs (1925-2015)

### Takeaway
Mattiwilda Dobbs was an Atlanta-born coloratura soprano and the daughter of John Wesley Dobbs. She won the 1951 Geneva competition and in 1953 became the first African American to sing a lead role at La Scala. She made her Met debut as Gilda in 1956, refused to sing for segregated audiences until Atlanta desegregated its auditorium in 1962, and later taught at the University of Texas and Howard. She died in Atlanta on December 8, 2015.

### Cited Findings
**Identity, birth, death**
- Mattiwilda Dobbs, later Mattiwilda Dobbs Janzon. Named for her maternal grandmother, Mattie Wilda Sykes — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Born July 11, 1925, in Atlanta — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Died December 8, 2015, at her Atlanta home — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/); [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
- Cause of death: "a brief bout with cancer" — [SYM, quoting AJC](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
  - **Minor conflict on place of death:** NGE and SYM say her home. A search summary of the Philadelphia Inquirer / Washington Post obituary says a "retirement community in Atlanta" — [Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/philly/news/20151214_Mattiwilda_Dobbs___Pioneering_soprano__90.html) (search summary only)

**Burial**
- A Toni Morrison Society page announcing its 2018 "Bench by the Road" at South-View lists Mattiwilda Dobbs among those interred there — [Toni Morrison Society](https://www.tonimorrisonsociety.org/twenty-third-bench-by-the-road.php) (search summary; page not opened)

**Family**
- Parents: John Wesley Dobbs and Irene Ophelia Thompson. She was the fifth of six daughters — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- First husband, Luis Rodriguez, died of a liver ailment in June 1954, about 14 months after their wedding — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Second husband, Swedish newspaperman Bengt Janzon, married just before Christmas 1957 in New York. He died in 1997 — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Nephew Maynard Jackson was Atlanta's first Black mayor. Survivors included her sister June Dobbs Butts — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)

**Education**
- Spelman College: music degree, valedictorian, class of 1946 — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
- Studied voice with Lotte Leonard in New York. Won a Marian Anderson Award and a John Hay Whitney Fellowship, which funded study in Europe — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Honorary doctorate from Spelman, 1979 — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)

**Timeline**
- 1946: Graduated from Spelman as valedictorian — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
- 1951: Won the International Music Competition in Geneva, Switzerland — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- 1953: Sang at La Scala in Milan, the first African American to do so — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/). The role was Elvira in Rossini's *L'italiana in Algeri*, the first lead role there by an African American — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
- 1954: U.S. debut. NGE says a New York recital — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/). SYM says with the Little Orchestra Society in New York — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
  - **Possible conflict:** a search summary attributes to the Washington Post a claim that her American (operatic) debut was at San Francisco Opera in 1955. This is unverified, and it may refer to her first U.S. staged opera rather than her first concert appearance.
- November 9, 1956: Met debut as Gilda in Verdi's *Rigoletto*. The year and role come from [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/) and [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/). The exact date comes from [bach-cantatas.com](https://bach-cantatas.com/Bio/Dobbs-Mattiwilda.htm) (search summary). A 1956 review by Douglas Watt in the AUC archive calls her the first Black singer to sing a romantic lead on the Met stage — [AUC RADAR clipping](https://radar.auctr.edu/_flysystem/repo-bin/2024-12/auc.090.0358.pdf) (search summary)
- She was the first Black woman offered a long-term Met contract, and sang 29 performances in six roles over eight seasons — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- She refused to perform for segregated audiences, following Paul Robeson's example — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/). As a result, she did not sing professionally in Atlanta until 1962 — [Inquirer obituary](https://inquirer.com/philly/news/20151214_Mattiwilda_Dobbs___Pioneering_soprano__90.html) (search summary)
- 1962: After the Atlanta Municipal Auditorium was desegregated, she sang for a large integrated audience. Mayor Ivan Allen Jr. joined her onstage and gave her a key to the city — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- 1974: Began teaching at the University of Texas, the first African American artist on its faculty. Spelman artist-in-residence, 1974-75. Later a professor of voice at Howard University (years not given) — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
- Gave recitals until at least 1990. Served on the Metropolitan Opera board and on the NEA Solo Recital Panel — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)

**Facts a visitor might ask about**
1. She was the first African American to sing a lead role at La Scala: Elvira, 1953 — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)
2. She would not sing in segregated Atlanta. Her 1962 homecoming concert was before an integrated audience, and the mayor gave her a key to the city — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
3. She was the first Black woman given a long-term Met contract, debuting as Gilda in 1956 — [NGE-MD](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/arts-culture/mattiwilda-dobbs-1925-2015/)
4. She was the daughter of John Wesley Dobbs and the aunt of Maynard Jackson — [SYM](https://symphony.org/obituary-soprano-mattiwilda-dobbs-90/)

### Inferences
- "First Black principal singer at La Scala" matches the sources' wording "first African American to perform a lead role" (SYM) and "first African American" to sing there (NGE). Sources say "African American" rather than "Black" in general, so the chatbot should use the sourced phrasing.

### Gaps
- No children are mentioned in any source consulted.
- South-View burial rests on a single non-news source (Toni Morrison Society). No obituary or funeral notice naming South-View was found, and no plot or burial date. Find a Grave was not checked.
- The New York Times obituary could not be retrieved. Graduate study (for example, Columbia Teachers College) and international engagements (Covent Garden, Glyndebourne and others) were not verified.

## Henry Louis "Hank" Aaron (1934-2021)

### Takeaway
Hank Aaron, born in Mobile, Alabama, started with the Negro Leagues' Indianapolis Clowns in 1952 and played for the Braves from 1954 to 1974. He broke Babe Ruth's record with home run No. 715 in Atlanta on April 8, 1974, finished with 755 HR and 2,297 RBI, entered the Hall of Fame in 1982, and later worked in the Braves front office. He died January 22, 2021, and after a January 27 funeral at Friendship Baptist Church was buried at South-View.

### Cited Findings
**Identity, birth, death**
- Henry Louis Aaron, nicknamed "Hammerin' Hank" — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/); [AP](https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2021/01/27/the-hammer-makes-one-last-trip-to-spot-where-he-hit-no-715/)
- Born February 5, 1934, in Mobile, Alabama, the third of eight children of Estella and Herbert Aaron — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/)
- Died January 22, 2021, in Atlanta — [HOF](https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/aaron-hank); [AJC-HA](https://www.ajc.com/news/obituaries/aaron-hank/GX3PZQY26ZALHN4RUXWM3PDPZA). He died of natural causes at 86 — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/). The Braves said he died peacefully in his sleep — [ABC/AP](https://abc7ny.com/sports/baseball-icon-hank-aaron-dies-at-86/9905703/) (search summary)

**Funeral and burial**
- Funeral: Wednesday, January 27, 2021, at Friendship Baptist Church, Atlanta. The service ran nearly three hours, with about 50 attendees because of COVID-19 limits — [AP](https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2021/01/27/the-hammer-makes-one-last-trip-to-spot-where-he-hit-no-715/)
- Speakers were former President Bill Clinton, former MLB Commissioner Bud Selig and Andrew Young. Former President Jimmy Carter sent a videotaped message — [AP](https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2021/01/27/the-hammer-makes-one-last-trip-to-spot-where-he-hit-no-715/)
- The hearse detoured through the former Atlanta-Fulton County Stadium site, where he hit No. 715, and went down Hank Aaron Drive. He was buried at South-View Cemetery, which AP calls "the oldest Black burial ground in Atlanta," also the resting place of John Lewis and Julian Bond — [AP](https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2021/01/27/the-hammer-makes-one-last-trip-to-spot-where-he-hit-no-715/)
- The Braves held a memorial at Truist Park the day before the funeral (Tuesday) — [AP](https://www.news4jax.com/sports/2021/01/27/the-hammer-makes-one-last-trip-to-spot-where-he-hit-no-715/)
  - **Possible discrepancy on interment:** a hobbyist site says his body was first placed in an existing vault at South-View, his permanent tomb five plots away was finished later, and a small re-interment ceremony took place on January 5, 2022 — [Kurt's Historic Sites](https://www.kurtshistoricsites.com/hank-aaron/) (low-authority source, search summary). AP reported a burial at South-View on January 27, 2021. The two accounts can both be true, but the 2022 re-interment is not corroborated by a major outlet.

**Family**
- Wife Billye. Children Gaile, Hank Jr., Lary, Dorinda and Ceci. Survived also by grandchildren — [AJC-HA](https://www.ajc.com/news/obituaries/aaron-hank/GX3PZQY26ZALHN4RUXWM3PDPZA)

**Education**

**Timeline**
- Spring 1952: Joined the Negro Leagues' Indianapolis Clowns at age 18 as a shortstop and led the league with a .467 average — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/)
- 1953: MVP of the minor-league Sally League — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/)
- 1954: MLB debut after the Boston Braves bought his Clowns contract. Played for the Milwaukee Braves 1954-1965, the Atlanta Braves 1966-1974 and the Milwaukee Brewers 1975-1976 — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/); [HOF](https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/aaron-hank)
- 1957: NL MVP (.322, 44 HR, 132 RBI) and World Series champion with the Braves — [HOF](https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/aaron-hank)
- Three Gold Gloves after 1957 — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/); [HOF](https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/aaron-hank)

- May 17, 1970: Became the first player with both 3,000 hits and 500 home runs — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/)
- April 8, 1974: Hit No. 715 at Atlanta-Fulton County Stadium off the Dodgers' Al Downing, passing Babe Ruth — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/); [HOF](https://baseballhall.org/hall-of-famers/aaron-hank)
- 1976: Retired as a player — [NGE-HA](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/sports-outdoor-recreation/hank-aaron-1934-2021/)
