## 1. Alonzo Franklin Herndon (1858–1927): founder of Atlanta Life Insurance Company

### Takeaway
Herndon was born enslaved in Walton County, Georgia. He became a barber, built a chain of upscale Atlanta barbershops, and in 1905 founded the company that became Atlanta Life Insurance Company. At his death in 1927 he was Atlanta's wealthiest Black citizen. He is buried at South-View Cemetery, and a Georgia Historical Society marker there names him.

### Cited Findings

**Identity, birth, death**
- Full name: Alonzo Franklin Herndon. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Born June 26, 1858, in Walton County, Georgia. He grew up on a farm in Social Circle, about 40 miles east of Atlanta. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Died in Atlanta on July 21, 1927, a few weeks after his 69th birthday. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Nickname: none found in the sources consulted.

**Burial**
- South-View Cemetery. The Georgia Historical Society marker (erected 2016 by GHS and the Historic South-View Preservation Foundation) names "Alonzo Herndon, founder of the Atlanta Life Insurance Company" among its notable burials. — [GHS-SouthView](https://www.georgiahistory.com/?p=38883)
- Wikipedia's South-View list also includes him (as "one of the first Black millionaires") and his first wife, Adrienne McNeil Herndon. It cites Patch.com (2011) and the Atlanta Preservation Center's "HerStories: The Women of South-View" (2013). — [WP-SouthView](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-View_Cemetery)
- Herndon supported the Southview Cemetery Association during his lifetime. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)

**Parents and family**
- Father: Frank Herndon, a white man who enslaved him. Mother: Sophenie, an enslaved woman. Younger brother: Thomas ("Tom"). His maternal grandparents lived with the family after emancipation. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- After emancipation his father sent the family away. At about age 7, Herndon, his mother, his brother and his grandparents were homeless and destitute. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- First wife: Adrienne Elizabeth McNeil, married 1893. She was a professor at Atlanta University, had studied dramatic arts in Boston and New York, received the Belasco Gold Medal in 1908, and died in 1910. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Second wife: Jessie Gillespie of Chicago, married after Adrienne's death. After Herndon died, she joined the Atlanta Life board, and she and his stepson took control of the company as major stockholders. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Only child: Norris Herndon, son of Adrienne. He succeeded his father as chief executive of Atlanta Life. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Norris Herndon ran Atlanta Life until his death in 1955. — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home)

**Education**
- He had about one year of schooling before leaving Social Circle in 1878, and he overcame illiteracy during his life. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)

**Career timeline** (all from [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/) unless noted)
- 1870s: As a boy, peddled peanuts, homemade molasses and axle grease to save money.
- 1878: Left Social Circle on foot with $11. Worked as a farmhand in Senoia (Coweta County) and began learning barbering.
- c. 1878–1883: Opened his first barbershop in Jonesboro (Clayton County) and stayed about five years.
- Early 1883: Moved to Atlanta to work in William Dougherty Hutchins's barbershop on Marietta Street. About six months later he bought a half interest in it.
- By 1904: Owned three Atlanta barbershops. The flagship, at 66 Peachtree Street, was advertised as the largest and best in the region.
- 1905: Bought a failing mutual aid association and incorporated it as the Atlanta Mutual Insurance Association, with himself as president and chief stockholder. Assets grew from $5,000 (1905) to more than $400,000 (1922).
- September 1906: During the Atlanta race massacre, a mob smashed the windows of his barbershop while he was at home. Black barbers in a shop across the street were killed.
- 1910: The Herndon Home was built for Alonzo and Adrienne Herndon at 587 University Place NW. — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home)
- 1922: The company was reorganized as the Atlanta Life Insurance Company and gained legal reserve status, which only four other Black insurers then held.
- 1920s: Atlanta Life expanded into Florida, Kansas, Kentucky, Missouri, Tennessee and Texas.
- He rescued failing Black businesses by reinsuring their policyholders and merging them into Atlanta Life.
- Wealth: He owned more than 100 houses, a large block of commercial property on Auburn Avenue, and an estate in Tavares, Florida. His real estate was assessed at nearly $325,000 at his death, and he was Atlanta's wealthiest Black citizen.

**Crystal Palace barbershop (FLAG: details not confirmed from a primary source)**
- NGE puts the flagship at 66 Peachtree Street by 1904 but does not use the name "Crystal Palace." — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
- Secondary sources call the 66 Peachtree shop the "Crystal Palace." They describe gold fixtures, marble floors and crystal chandeliers, with Black barbers serving an exclusively white clientele of lawyers, judges, politicians and businessmen. — [Edwardian Promenade, "Alonzo Herndon and his Crystal Palace"](https://edwardianpromenade.com/african-american/alonzo-herndon-and-his-crystal-palace); [Rough Draft Atlanta, 2018](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2018/08/01/perspectives-in-architecture-a-historical-gem-in-the-community/)
- CONFLICT: These secondary sources disagree on when the shop opened as the Crystal Palace. One says it opened in 1902; another says the Herndon barbershop had become the Crystal Palace by spring 1913. I could not confirm which source gives which date. Do not state an opening year without checking.

**Civil rights and civic activity** ([NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/))
- 1900: Was among the Atlanta delegates at the founding meeting of Booker T. Washington's National Negro Business League in Boston.
- 1905: Was one of the 29 men at the founding meeting of W. E. B. Du Bois's Niagara Movement.
- Supported the YMCA; Atlanta University; the Leonard Street, Carrie Steele and Diana Pace orphanages; the Herndon Day Nursery; First Congregational Church; the Southview Cemetery Association; and Atlanta State Savings Bank.

**Honors, memorials, legacy**
- Herndon Home: 587 University Place NW, built in 1910 in the Classical Revival style with Beaux Arts influences. Much of the design is credited to Adrienne Herndon. African American craftsmen did almost all of the work except the electrical and plumbing. — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home)
- Designations: Atlanta Landmark Building (Oct. 14, 1989); National Register of Historic Places and National Historic Landmark (Feb. 16, 2000). — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home) (citing the [NHL nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/00000261_text))
- The Alonzo F. and Norris B. Herndon Foundation owns the home, has run it as a museum since 1983, and supports entrepreneurship. — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home)
- The home sits on Diamond Hill in Atlanta's Historic Westside. The foundation runs youth entrepreneurship programming, including the "Game Changer" program for 11th and 12th graders, which began in 2013. — [Rough Draft Atlanta](https://roughdraftatlanta.com/2018/08/01/perspectives-in-architecture-a-historical-gem-in-the-community/)
- Atlanta Life survived into the 21st century and is regularly ranked among the top Black financial companies by *Black Enterprise*. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)

**Visitor-facing notable facts**
1. Born enslaved in 1858, he left home at 20 with $11 and became Atlanta's wealthiest Black citizen. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
2. A mob attacked his barbershop during the 1906 Atlanta race massacre. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
3. He attended the founding of both Booker T. Washington's National Negro Business League (1900) and Du Bois's Niagara Movement (1905). — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
4. His 1905 insurance association grew from $5,000 to over $400,000 in assets by 1922 and became Atlanta Life. — [NGE-Herndon](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/alonzo-herndon-1858-1927/)
5. His Herndon Home (1910) is a National Historic Landmark and museum. His first wife, Adrienne, is credited with much of its design and is also buried at South-View. — [WP-HerndonHome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herndon_Home); [WP-SouthView](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-View_Cemetery)

### Inferences
- The Herndon Home was finished in 1910, the same year NGE gives for Adrienne's death, so she likely lived there only briefly. No fetched source states this directly.

### Gaps
- No exact death date found for Adrienne in 1910 (NGE gives only the year). No birth year found for Norris Herndon, and no source confirms where Norris is buried.
- No marriage year found for Jessie Gillespie, and the stepson is not named.
- The NPS travel page (https://www.nps.gov/nr/Travel/atlanta/her.htm) returned a 404, and the NHL nomination PDF is a scanned image that could not be read, so the Herndon Home facts come from Wikipedia's summary of the NHL documents.
- "Atlanta's first Black millionaire" appears in secondary sources (e.g., Rough Draft Atlanta). NGE says only "wealthiest Black citizen."

## 2. Jesse Hill Jr. (1926/1927–2012): president of Atlanta Life, civil rights and civic leader

### Takeaway
Hill was an actuary from St. Louis. He joined Atlanta Life in 1949 and in 1973 became its first president outside the Herndon family. He co-founded the *Atlanta Inquirer* (1960), led voter registration drives, was the first Black member of the Georgia Board of Regents, and became the first Black head of the Atlanta Chamber of Commerce. Find a Grave and Wikipedia's South-View list (citing Henderson 2018) place his burial at South-View. Sources disagree on his birth year, his retirement date and the year he took the Chamber post.

### Cited Findings

**Identity, birth, death**
- Name: Jesse Hill Jr. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- Born in St. Louis, Missouri. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)
- CONFLICT on birth date: NGE gives the year as **1927** (title "1927–2012"). Find a Grave gives **May 30, 1926**, as does Wikipedia (lead only). Age 86 at a December 2012 death fits 1926. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [FAG-Hill](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/102285243/jesse-hill); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Hill)
- Died December 17, 2012, at age 86. Wikipedia gives Atlanta as the place of death. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Hill)
- Funeral at Big Bethel AME Church, at Auburn Avenue and Jesse Hill Jr. Drive. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)

**Burial**
- South-View Cemetery, Atlanta (Find a Grave memorial 102285243, birth May 30, 1926). — [FAG-Hill](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/102285243/jesse-hill)
- Wikipedia's South-View list includes him as "former president of the Atlanta Life Insurance Company and civil rights activist," citing Henderson 2018, p. 6. — [WP-SouthView](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-View_Cemetery)
- The GHS marker does not name him. — [GHS-SouthView](https://www.georgiahistory.com/?p=38883)

**Family**
- Parents: Nancy Dennis Martin and Jesse Hill. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- Married Azira Gonzalez in 1955. Daughters: Nancy Mercedes and Azira Dominga. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)

**Education and military service**
- Lincoln University, 1947: bachelor's degree in mathematics and physics. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- CONFLICT on location: NGE places Lincoln University in St. Louis, while AP says Jefferson City, Missouri (where Lincoln University is located). — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)
- University of Michigan, 1949: NGE says MBA; AP says master's in actuarial science (FLAG). He later received an honorary doctor of laws from Michigan in the 1990s. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)
- Served in the Korean War, 1952–1954. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)

**Career timeline**
- 1949: Moved to Atlanta and joined Atlanta Life as assistant actuary, one of the few African American actuaries in the country. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- 1949–1973: Rose to vice president and chief actuary. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- 1960: Helped create the *Atlanta Inquirer*, described as "the city's first newspaper for the African American community." Served as publisher until 1985. — [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html) (NGE does not mention the *Inquirer*.)
- 1973: Elected president and CEO of Atlanta Life, the company's third president and the first not related to Alonzo Herndon. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- 1973–1984: First African American member of the Georgia Board of Regents. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- 1977 or 1978: Head of the Atlanta Chamber of Commerce. CONFLICT: NGE says he became chairman in 1977 and describes him as the first African American elected president of a major-city chamber; AP says he was named head in 1978 as its "first black president." — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)
- 1979–1993: Chairman of the King Center board, per AP. — [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html). FLAG: another AP version (The Root) reportedly ends the term in 1995. — [The Root](https://www.theroot.com/civil-rights-leader-jesse-hill-jr-dead-at-86-1790894555)
- Retirement from Atlanta Life. CONFLICT: NGE says 1995; AP says 1990; Wikipedia says he served as CEO 1973–1992; the Auburn Avenue Research Library finding aid reportedly says 1995. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012); [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Hill); [AARL finding aid](https://aafa.galileo.usg.edu/repositories/2/resources/292)
- Other roles: Served on the boards of eight major corporations, including Knight Ridder, Delta Air Lines, National Service Industries and SunTrust Banks. He was a founding director of MARTA and served on the board for the 1996 Atlanta Olympics, which he helped bring to the city. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
- Served on the board of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference. — [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)

**Civil rights and civic activity** ([NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012) unless noted)
- 1950s–1960s: Collected donations from Atlanta Life employees for Martin Luther King Jr.'s efforts.
- 1960: Helped produce "A Second Look," a survey of Atlanta's Black population, with the Atlanta Committee for Cooperative Action.
- NAACP education committee: Recruited students to desegregate Georgia colleges, which led to the admission of Hamilton Holmes and Charlayne Hunter to the University of Georgia.
- Late 1960s–1970s: Led voter registration drives that added about 50,000 new Black voters in Atlanta.
- Ran campaigns for Maynard Jackson (Atlanta's first Black mayor) and Andrew Young.
- 2003: Co-chaired the committee to rename Hartsfield International Airport for Maynard Jackson.
- Had a "close relationship" with Martin Luther King Jr. and helped ensure his legacy would be remembered, according to the King Center. — [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)

**Honors and legacy** ([NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012))
- 2001: Butler Street in Atlanta was renamed **Jesse Hill Jr. Drive**.
- 2007: Received Atlanta Gas Light Company's Shining Light Award.
- Credited with expanding African American access to affordable home mortgages in Georgia, Alabama, Texas and Florida.
- His papers are held at the Auburn Avenue Research Library. — [AARL finding aid](https://aafa.galileo.usg.edu/repositories/2/resources/292)

**Visitor-facing notable facts**
1. He was the first Atlanta Life president not related to founder Alonzo Herndon, who is buried in the same cemetery. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
2. Jesse Hill Jr. Drive (formerly Butler Street, renamed 2001) runs beside Big Bethel AME, where his funeral was held. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
3. He co-founded the *Atlanta Inquirer* in 1960 and was its publisher until 1985. — [AP-Hill](https://www.inquirer.com/philly/obituaries/20121220_Jesse_Hill_Jr____Atlanta_civil_rights_leader__86.html)
4. He recruited the students who desegregated the University of Georgia (Hamilton Holmes and Charlayne Hunter). — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)
5. He was the first Black member of the Georgia Board of Regents (1973–1984) and the first Black head of the Atlanta Chamber of Commerce. — [NGE-Hill](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/jesse-hill-1927-2012)

### Inferences
- The 1926 birth year fits better with his reported age of 86 at a December 2012 death. NGE's "1927" may be an error, but this is not confirmed.

### Gaps
- No New York Times or AJC obituary was retrieved. Hill's cause of death was not found.
- No source states his exact title at the *Atlanta Inquirer* beyond "publisher," and the other co-founders are not named in the sources fetched.

## 3. Herman Jerome Russell (1930–2014): founder of H.J. Russell & Company

### Takeaway
Russell grew up in Summerhill, Atlanta, and trained at Tuskegee. He took over his father's plastering business and built H.J. Russell & Company into what was described in the early 2000s as the largest minority-owned real estate and construction firm in the U.S. His firm worked on Hartsfield-Jackson airport, the Georgia Dome, Turner Field and Philips Arena. He quietly funded the civil rights movement and was the first Black member of the Atlanta Chamber of Commerce. He is buried at South-View.

### Cited Findings

**Identity, birth, death**
- Full name: Herman Jerome Russell. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- Born December 23, 1930, in Atlanta, the youngest of eight children of Maggie Googson and Rogers Russell. He grew up in the Summerhill neighborhood. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- Died November 15, 2014, in Atlanta, at age 83, after a "brief illness" (per Atlanta Black Star). — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/); [ABS-Russell](https://atlantablackstar.com/2014/11/17/atlanta-icon-herman-j-russell-83-passed-away/)
- Overcame a severe childhood speech impediment. — [ABS-Russell](https://atlantablackstar.com/2014/11/17/atlanta-icon-herman-j-russell-83-passed-away/)

**Burial**
- South-View Cemetery. CBS News Atlanta (Feb. 2, 2026) notes "the burial site of Herman J. Russell, a major Atlanta developer" there. — [CBS-SouthView](https://www.cbsnews.com/atlanta/news/south-view-cemetery-ensures-over-a-century-of-black-atlantas-history-isnt-forgotten/)
- Wikipedia's South-View list includes him as "entrepreneur, civic leader, and philanthropist," citing Henderson 2018, p. 239. — [WP-SouthView](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South-View_Cemetery)

**Family**
- Married Otelia Hackney in 1956. She was from Union Point, had graduated from Clark College, and died in 2006. Their children are Donata, H. Jerome and Michael. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- Later married Sylvia Anderson, described as a president at AT&T. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- 2015: Joycelyn Darby Alston claimed to be his daughter, and DNA tests confirmed it. She declined a settlement, and legal proceedings followed; the family has not acknowledged her as an heir. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- His son H. Jerome Russell is the current president of H.J. Russell & Company (as of 2026). — [AT-RussellHQ](https://atlantatribune.com/2026/05/06/former-h-j-russell-company-headquarters-named-georgia-historical-site/)

**Education**
- David T. Howard High School, Atlanta. Degree in building construction from Tuskegee Institute (now Tuskegee University); no year given. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)

**Career timeline**
- As a high school sophomore, bought his first property, which he later developed to pay for college. — [NGE-Russell](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/business-economy/herman-j-russell-1930-2014/)
- Founding date CONFLICT: Atlanta Black Star says the company was founded in 1952. The Atlanta Tribune quotes H. Jerome Russell saying his father was 22 when he bought the land for the original building (about 1952–53) and calls the firm nearly 75 years old in 2026.
