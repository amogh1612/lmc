## Julian Bond (1940–2015): identity, burial status, family, education, career, legacy

### Takeaway
Horace Julian Bond co-founded SNCC, won a Georgia House seat in 1965 that the House refused to give him until the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in 1966, was the first president of the SPLC (1971–1979), and chaired the NAACP from 1998. **Burial status conflicts.** Press reports say his family scattered his ashes over the Gulf of Mexico. Find a Grave nonetheless lists a South-View Cemetery plot, and the 2016 GHS marker does not name him among South-View's notable burials. No source found explains what, if anything, is physically at South-View.

## Grace Towns Hamilton (1907–1992): identity, burial, family, education, career, legacy

### Takeaway
An Atlanta native, Hamilton led the Atlanta Urban League from 1943 to 1961. In 1965 she became the first African American woman elected to the Georgia General Assembly and the first Black woman in the Deep South elected to a state legislature. She was the principal architect of the 1973 Atlanta City Charter and served until her 1984 defeat. Her South-View burial is reported by Wikipedia (citing *Notable American Women*) and a Kiddle mirror; no Find a Grave memorial or other primary source was found.

## Bishop Henry McNeal Turner (1834–1915): identity, burial, family, career, legacy

### Takeaway
Turner was born free in South Carolina. He became the first Black chaplain in the U.S. Army (1863), was a Reconstruction Georgia legislator expelled in 1868, and in 1880 became the 12th bishop of the AME Church. He was a leading advocate of emigration to Africa. He died in Windsor, Ontario, in 1915, and his burial at South-View Cemetery is confirmed by the GHS marker and Find a Grave.

### Cited Findings

**Identity, birth and death**
- NGE gives his birth as 1834 at Newberry Courthouse, South Carolina, with no exact date. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
  - Find a Grave gives February 1, 1834, in Newberry, S.C. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
  - CONFLICT: Mother Bethel AME gives February 1, 1834, "near Abbeville, South Carolina." — [Mother Bethel AME](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)
- Turner was never enslaved. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- He died May 8, 1915, in Windsor, Ontario, Canada, while traveling on church business. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/); [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- Cause of death: a stroke, per Find a Grave. A search summary (not a direct read) said he suffered a massive stroke as he disembarked the ferry at Windsor. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- An estimated 25,000 people attended his funeral in Atlanta. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222) (low-authority source; corroborate before relying on it)

**Burial (confirmed)**
- The GHS marker (erected 2016) lists "Bishop Henry McNeal Turner, Reconstruction-era state legislator and African Methodist Episcopal Church organizer" among South-View's notable burials. — [GHS marker](https://georgiahistory.com/ghmi_marker_updated/south-view-cemetery/)
- Find a Grave memorial #14775222 places him at South-View Cemetery, Atlanta. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- NGE says only that he was buried in Atlanta. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)

**Family**
- Parents: Sarah Greer and Hardy Turner. His paternal grandmother was a white plantation owner. Family legend says his maternal grandfather, David Greer, arrived on a slave ship. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- He married four times:
  - Eliza Peacher, married 1856, died 1889. She was the daughter of a wealthy Black builder in Columbia, S.C. They had 14 children, 4 of whom survived to adulthood.
  - Martha Elizabeth DeWitt, married 1893.
  - Harriet A. Wayman, married 1900.
  - Laura Pearl Lemon, married 1907. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
  - Mother Bethel spells the first wife's name "Eliza Ann Preacher." — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)
- Find a Grave lists children Dr. John Payne Turner (1859–1924), David M. Turner (1862–1927), and Lincolnia Victoria Turner King (1864–1892). — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)

**Education**
- Around age 15 he worked as a janitor at an Abbeville, S.C., law firm, and the lawyers helped educate him. NGE describes no formal degrees. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)

**Career timeline**
- 1853: Licensed to preach. He then worked as an itinerant evangelist across the South, including Macon, Athens, Atlanta, and New Orleans. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1858: Moved to St. Louis and joined the AME Church. He then held pastorates in Baltimore and Washington, D.C., where he befriended Charles Sumner and Thaddeus Stevens. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1863: Helped organize the 1st Regiment, U.S. Colored Troops in his churchyard and was mustered in as its chaplain. He served in Virginia-theater battles. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
  - Mother Bethel says he became "the first Black chaplain in the U.S. Army." — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)
  - A search summary attributed the same claim to BlackPast; that page could not be loaded. Another search summary described him as the first of 14 Black chaplains appointed during the Civil War.
- 1865: Resigned a reassigned chaplaincy in Atlanta and began organizing AME churches across Georgia. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1867: Helped organize Georgia's Republican Party, served in the state constitutional convention, and was elected to the Georgia House from Macon. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
  - CONFLICT: Mother Bethel and Find a Grave say he was elected in 1868. — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/); [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- 1868: The Black legislators were expelled. Turner spoke against the expulsion, without success, and afterward received Ku Klux Klan threats. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
  - Mother Bethel says Turner and 14 other Black representatives were expelled on a vote it gives as "82-83." These figures look garbled and should not be used. — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)
  - Find a Grave is internally inconsistent: it gives both "twenty-five" and "26 other" expelled legislators. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- 1869: President Grant appointed him postmaster of Macon. He resigned weeks later amid allegations. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1870: Reseated in the legislature at Congress's urging, then lost a contested reelection months later. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- After 1870: Worked at the Savannah Custom House and pastored St. Philip's AME Church. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1876: Elected manager of the AME publishing house. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1880: Elected the 12th bishop of the AME Church at the General Conference in St. Louis. He was the first Southern bishop elected. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/); [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)
- Morris Brown College (Atlanta): Mother Bethel says he served 12 years as chancellor. Find a Grave says he "helped found" the college. A search summary (page not loaded) said BlackPast calls him its president from 1876. — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/); [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)
- 1885: Became the first AME bishop to ordain a woman, Sarah Ann Hughes, as deacon. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1891–1898: Made four trips to Africa and organized AME conferences in Liberia and Sierra Leone. He also helped merge the AME Church with the Ethiopian Church in South Africa, and sent missionaries to Cuba and Mexico. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- 1890s: Organized the International Migration Society with white Alabama businessmen. In 1895 and 1896, two ships carried 500 or more emigrants to Liberia; some later returned. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- He led protests against segregated Atlanta streetcars. — [FaG](https://www.findagrave.com/memorial/14775222)

**Publications and editorships**
- *The Genius and Theory of Methodist Polity* (1885). — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- Editor of *The Voice of Missions* (1893–1900) and *The Voice of the People* (1901–1904). — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- He is associated with the declaration "God is a Negro." Mother Bethel places it at "the first Black Baptist convention" and gives no date. This attribution is doubtful for an AME bishop and should be verified before use. — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/)

**Honors and legacy**
- His portrait hangs in the Georgia State Capitol. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
- The 2016 GHS South-View marker names him. — [GHS marker](https://georgiahistory.com/ghmi_marker_updated/south-view-cemetery/)

**Notable visitor-facing facts**
1. He was the first Black chaplain in the U.S. Army (1863, 1st U.S. Colored Troops). — [Mother Bethel](https://motherbethel.org/henry-mcneal-turner/); [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
2. He was expelled from the Georgia legislature with the other Black members in 1868 and reseated in 1870. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
3. As the AME Church's 12th bishop, he was the first to ordain a woman as deacon (1885). — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
4. He championed emigration to Liberia, and ships sailed under his International Migration Society in 1895 and 1896. — [NGE](https://www.georgiaencyclopedia.org/articles/history-archaeology/henry-mcneal-turner-1834-1915/)
5. He died in Canada in 1915 and is buried at South-View, named on the cemetery's 2016 state historical marker. — [GHS marker](https://georgiahistory.com/ghmi_marker_updated/south-view-cemetery/)

### Inferences
- The "first Black Army chaplain" claim is widely repeated. The NGE describes only the 1863 appointment and does not state the "first" explicitly, so the chatbot should phrase it as "widely recognized as the first."

### Gaps

- His 1883 response to the Supreme Court's Civil Rights Cases decision is not covered. Pages that might have covered it (BlackPast, UNC DocSouth, Britannica) were unreachable.
- His Freedmen's Bureau service was not covered in any source retrieved.
